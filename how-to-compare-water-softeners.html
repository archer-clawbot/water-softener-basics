How to Compare Water Softener Systems

"Best softener" lists rarely tell you much, because the right unit depends on your water, your household and the support available where you live. A more useful approach is to compare a few systems side by side on the same criteria.

1. Type of treatment

Decide first whether you need hardness removed (ion exchange) or something else entirely. If your test shows iron, sulfur smell, bacteria or other contaminants, a softener may be only part of the answer. Water System Guide's overview of how to choose a home water treatment system is a good place to sort that out.

2. Capacity for your home

Compare units based on the capacity you actually need, not the biggest number on the box. Work out your rough requirement first, then ask each seller to show how their recommended size fits your hardness and usage.

3. Regeneration method

Timer-based units regenerate on a schedule; demand-initiated (metered) units regenerate based on water used. Ask how each system you're comparing decides when to regenerate and how much salt and water a regeneration uses.

4. Control valve and parts

The control valve is the part most likely to need service. Ask which valve the system uses, whether replacement parts are readily available, and whether local plumbers can service it or only the original dealer.

5. Warranty, in writing

Warranty terms vary by manufacturer and dealer. Get answers in writing: what's covered on the tank, resin and valve, for how long, and does it cover labor or parts only?

6. Total cost of ownership

Look beyond the purchase price. Ask each dealer what installation includes, what salt and maintenance will look like for your usage, and whether any service plan is optional.

Use a simple table with these six rows and one column per system. The comparison usually becomes clear quickly.
