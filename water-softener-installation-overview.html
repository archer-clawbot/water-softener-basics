Water Softener Installation: What the Job Involves

Installing a softener is a plumbing job. Some homeowners do it themselves; many hire a plumber or the dealer. Either way, it helps to understand the main pieces so you can judge a quote or plan the work. Local plumbing codes apply, so check whether a permit or licensed plumber is required where you live.

Choosing a location

A softener usually goes near where the main water line enters the home, after any well pressure tank and before the water heater. It needs a level spot, protection from freezing, a nearby drain for regeneration water and, for most units, an electrical outlet. Outdoor hose bibs are often left on unsoftened water.

Main components of the job

  • Bypass valve: lets you route water around the softener for service.
  • Inlet and outlet connections: tying the unit into the main line in the right direction.
  • Drain line: carries regeneration water to a drain, with an air gap where code requires one.
  • Brine tank overflow: a separate line in case the salt tank overfills.

After installation

The unit has to be programmed with your water hardness and settings, and the resin is usually put through an initial regeneration. Ask the installer to show you how to bypass the system, add salt and check the settings.

DIY or hire it out?

If you're comfortable cutting into supply lines and soldering or using push-fit fittings, and your local code allows it, DIY is possible. If not, ask any installer to put in writing exactly what's included. For more background on softeners and other treatment options, visit https://watersystemguide.com/.
